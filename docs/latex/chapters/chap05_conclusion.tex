\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

The internship project implemented an intent-aware profile and image recommendation workflow for JupyterHub. It extracts structured workload intent, retrieves catalog candidates through sparse and dense channels, applies deterministic hard constraints, and presents the result for user review and confirmation. The administrator-managed catalog and pre-spawn validation keep deployment choices within approved profiles and digest-pinned images.

E1 evaluated recommendation quality on 11 workload families with effective paired outcomes from the broader development benchmark. JointAccept@1 was 60.0\% for P1, 65.0\% for P2 local, and 55.9\% for P2 LLM; the observed differences were not statistically significant. The retrieval ablation also showed that BM25 achieved higher Recall@1 (46.4\%) than hybrid RRF (34.1\%) and dense retrieval (24.3\%) on this catalog. These results do not establish that the hybrid method is superior; they show a trade-off that should be assessed against the query and catalog distribution. E2 measured prompt robustness across 18 semantic families, with macro SRR of 56.8\% for P1, 60.6\% for P2 local, and 51.5\% for P2 LLM.

E3 has a completed human-study protocol and testing harness but zero participant observations, so this report makes no empirical claim about user success, decision time, workload, or usability. In E4, 770 of 800 Pod trials succeeded (96.3\%), with 30 OOM terminations and no timeouts across four allocation conditions in one OrbStack-backed cluster. E5 found that the four-image catalog covered 69.9\% of cases for P1 and 84.3\% for P2; all executed eligible probes passed (65/65 and 76/76). These observations support the bounded implementation and the specific tests performed. They do not establish broad production performance, human effectiveness, or scalability to larger image catalogs.

\section{Future work}

The experimental results are subject to several limitations. The evaluation is based on a controlled resource and image catalog and therefore does not cover the full diversity of workloads, Kubernetes clusters, and software environments encountered in production. The human-study protocol has not yet been run with participants; future work should conduct the study and report results from the recruited participant population. In addition, image functionality depends on the specific images and probe requirements included in the evaluation. Future work can extend the evaluation to larger and more heterogeneous catalogs, additional workload domains, and longer-term user studies. Historical workload information could also be incorporated to improve recommendations based on previously observed resource usage. Further work on retrieval and ranking could investigate how recommendation quality changes as the catalog becomes larger and more diverse. Language-model-based re-ranking could also be explored as an optional additional stage after retrieval, provided that its computational cost and effect on recommendation quality are evaluated systematically. Finally, runtime resource measurements could be used to calibrate recommended resource allocations against actual workload behavior.
